      About the Journal

      Slow writing about rooms that last.

      Gilded Room Theory is an independent editorial publication exploring interior design, architecture, materials, and the art of living well within built space. We publish long-form essays and practical guides for people who believe the built environment shapes how life feels.

      Gilded Room Theory was founded in 2019 in a small studio in London, with a simple conviction: that the conversation about design had become too fast, too visual, and too disconnected from the experience of actually living in a space. We wanted to create a publication that treated architecture and interiors with the seriousness they deserve—not as lifestyle decoration, but as a fundamental part of how human beings make sense of the world.

      Our name is a provocation. "Gilded" suggests the ornamental and the decorative—the surfaces that catch the light. "Room Theory" suggests the analytical and the structural—the ideas that hold a space together. We believe both matter. A room without thought is hollow. A room without beauty is a diagram. The best rooms are both.

      "The rooms we remember are not the ones that photographed best. They are the ones that felt most themselves."

      What We Publish

      We publish three kinds of writing:

      • Critical essays that examine the ideas, histories, and cultural forces that shape the built environment—from the legacy of modernism to the politics of housing to the meaning of material honesty.
      • Practical guides that help people make better decisions about their own spaces, whether they are renovating a kitchen, planning a lighting scheme, or simply trying to make a small apartment feel like home.
      • Material studies that look closely at the substances we build with—timber, stone, plaster, glass, metal—and the ways they age, perform, and shape the experience of a room.

      We do not publish sponsored content. We do not use affiliate links. We do not accept payment for coverage. Our only allegiance is to our readers and to the truth as we understand it.

      How We Work

      Every article we publish goes through a deliberate, slow process. We research deeply. We visit buildings. We interview architects, craftspeople, historians, and homeowners. We argue with each other about drafts. We cut sentences we love because they do not serve the piece. We believe that writing about the built environment requires the same care that goes into building well—attention to detail, respect for materials, and a willingness to revise.

      Our editorial team is small and deliberately so. We are architects, writers, and critics who have spent years working in and thinking about the built environment. We are not influencers. We are not trend forecasters. We are people who care about how spaces work and why they matter.

      Our Values

      • 01 Material honesty. We believe in materials that are what they appear to be—that age with grace, can be repaired, and carry the marks of their making and use. We are skeptical of simulations and suspicious of the seamless.
      • 02 Slow looking. A building is not an image. It is a sequence of experiences, a set of relationships between body and space. We try to write in a way that honors that complexity, rather than reducing architecture to a photograph.
      • 03 Practical intelligence. We are interested in ideas that can be applied—in guides that help people make better decisions, in criticism that illuminates rather than obscures, in writing that is rigorous without being academic.
      • 04 Independence. We answer to no advertiser, no brand, no institution. Our only obligation is to our readers and to the integrity of the work.

      Editor-in-Chief

      Elena is a practicing architect and writer. She founded Gilded Room Theory in 2019 to create a space for slow, serious writing about the built environment. Her work has appeared in Architectural Review, Domus, and The Plan Journal. She lives in London.

      Marcus Chen
      Senior Editor, Practical Guides

      Marcus is an interior designer and educator. He writes about space planning, renovation, and the everyday decisions that shape how a home functions. He has taught at the Royal College of Art and runs a small practice in Brooklyn.

      Sofia Andersson
      Contributing Editor, Lighting & Interiors

      Sofia is a lighting designer and writer based in Stockholm. She writes about the ways light shapes atmosphere, behavior, and the experience of home. Her work focuses on the intersection of technical precision and emotional effect.

      James Okonkwo
      Contributing Editor, Architecture & Materials

      James is an architectural historian and critic. He writes about the legacies of modernism, the politics of materials, and the relationship between buildings and the landscapes they inhabit. He teaches at the University of Cape Town.
